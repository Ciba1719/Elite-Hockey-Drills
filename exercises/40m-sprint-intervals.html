i.

How to Do It

Mark a 40m course. Stand at the start. On your signal, sprint the full 40m at 95-100% effort. Time every rep. Target: maintain within 5% of best time across all reps. Walk or jog back to start — approximately 60-90 seconds rest. Repeat 6-10 times. Stop the session if times drop more than 10% from your best.

ii.

Why It Works

40-metre sprint intervals train the full acceleration-to-near-top-speed spectrum — the distance range of most meaningful on-ice sequences. From a defensive zone start to the opposing blue line is approximately 30-40 metres. Training this distance repeatedly develops the neuromuscular patterns, glycolytic capacity, and acceleration endurance that sustain explosive play through multiple consecutive shifts.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Conditions the anaerobic system for repeated high-speed shifts. Sprint posture stays tall the whole way. The second your shoulders round forward, decelerate and walk it in.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Max effort every rep"
  • "Full recovery between reps"
  • "Consistent mechanics"
v.

Common Mistakes

Starting at 70-80% — every rep must be near-maximal. Rest too long — 60-90 second recovery is deliberately incomplete. Shorter than full recovery. Not recording times — the data tells you when to stop and how you're progressing. Running beyond 10 reps — quality degrades rapidly. Stop at 10 maximum.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Shorter rest (45s) once times stay consistent with 90s rest.

Regression

20m sprint intervals — shorter distance for athletes building sprint capacity.
