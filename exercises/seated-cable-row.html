Seated Cable Row

i.

How to Do It

Sit at the cable row station, feet on the platform, knees slightly bent. Grip the handle — narrow (V-grip) for more lat; wide for more rear delt. Sit tall, chest up. Pull the handle to your lower sternum — elbows drive back. Squeeze shoulder blades together at full contraction. Hold 1 second. Extend arms fully under control — feel the stretch in the lats. Don't round the back to reach further — stop where your spine stays neutral.

ii.

Why It Works

The seated cable row builds the horizontal pulling strength that dominates puck battles — the mid-back, lats, and rear delts all working together to control an opponent or protect the puck. Unlike barbell rows, the cable keeps constant tension throughout the full range of motion, making it superior for building the thick mid-back that hockey players need for durability.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Constant tension on back muscles — excellent for mid-back thickness. Cable row is the safest heavy-pull variant for anyone with a cranky lower back. Same lat stimulus, no spine load.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Sit tall"
  • "Pull handle to belly button"
  • "Squeeze shoulder blades"
  • "Slow release"
v.

Common Mistakes

Using torso momentum to heave the weight — stay upright, pull with the back. Pulling to the belly button instead of the lower chest — shortens lat engagement. Not reaching full arm extension — the stretch is where you build the range. Shrugging the traps — pull shoulder blades DOWN and BACK, not up.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Single-arm cable row — unilateral loading increases core anti-rotation demand.

Regression

Band row — same movement with less load and no stability requirement.
