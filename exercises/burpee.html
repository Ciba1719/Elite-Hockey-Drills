i.

How to Do It

Stand with feet shoulder-width. Drop both hands to the floor just outside your feet. Jump both feet back simultaneously into a high plank position. Perform a full push-up — chest to floor, elbows at 45°. Jump feet back in toward your hands. Explode upward — full jump with arms overhead. Land softly and immediately begin the next rep.

ii.

Why It Works

The burpee trains the full-body metabolic demand of repeated explosive efforts — getting up off the ice after a hit, accelerating after a fall, or pushing through the third period when everything hurts. It combines a push-up (upper body), a squat thrust (hip flexors/core), and a vertical jump (power) into one continuous movement. Used correctly, it builds game-specific cardiovascular resilience.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Full-body conditioning with no equipment — builds game-ready fitness anywhere. If the push-up phase looks like a chest-flop, slow down and reset form. Bad burpees train bad mechanics.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Squat down"
  • "Jump feet back"
  • "Push-up"
  • "Jump feet in"
  • "Explosive jump at top"
v.

Common Mistakes

Sagging hips in the plank — maintain a rigid core throughout. Half-push-up (chest never touches) — go full depth or drop to knees. Barely leaving the ground on the jump — the jump must be maximal. Using burpees as pure conditioning at high reps with no quality control — form degrades and injury risk rises.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Burpee broad jump (jump forward instead of vertical) — adds horizontal power demand.

Regression

Step-out burpee (step feet back instead of jumping) — reduces impact for beginners.
