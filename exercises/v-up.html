V-Up

i.

How to Do It

Lie flat on your back, arms extended overhead, legs straight together. Press your lower back into the floor — that's the start position, no arch. Simultaneously and explosively raise both your torso AND your straight legs toward each other. Reach your hands toward your toes at the top — body forms a V shape. Lower with control back to the start position. Touch the floor lightly between reps; don't relax. 10-15 reps for a working set.

ii.

Why It Works

The V-up is one of the few dynamic spinal-flexion exercises still defensible for athletes. HIGH-volume LOADED repeated flexion (i.e. weighted crunches, 50+ rep sets) is what wrecks spines, but a low-volume bodyweight V-up trains explosive trunk-flexion power — a real hockey quality. The exact muscle action of a V-up — torso and legs explosively meeting at the top — is what a player does to get up off the ice after a fall, or to sit up into a check with the trunk braced. Programmed at 10-15 reps for 3 sets, 1-2× per week, the V-up adds a useful dynamic to a spine-safe core block without the disc-load downside of high-rep crunches.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Dynamic, explosive trunk-flexion under control — the exact muscle action of getting up off the ice fast after a fall, or sitting up into a check. Low-volume, bodyweight-only keeps it spine-safe for athletes. Bent knees on the way up = cheating. If you can't keep them straight, sub a tuck-up until you can.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Lie flat on your back"
  • "Arms overhead"
  • "Legs straight. Explosively raise torso AND straight legs simultaneously to meet at the top in a V. Reach hands toward toes. Lower with control."
v.

Common Mistakes

Bending the knees on the way up — kills the difficulty and the stimulus; straight legs or sub tuck-ups. Letting the lower back arch as legs lower — press it into the floor through the descent. Using arm momentum to fling up — both ends move together; not arms first. Treating it as high-volume cardio — keep sets ≤15 reps and quality high. This is strength work.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Weighted V-up — hold a light dumbbell or medicine ball overhead. Or candlestick V-up (legs go above vertical at top).

Regression

Tuck-up — same setup, bend knees as you rise. Build up to 15 clean tucks before progressing.
