Prone Hip Extension

i.

How to Do It

Lie face down on the floor, arms under your forehead. Squeeze the RIGHT glute first — before any movement. Lift the right leg off the floor with the knee straight — only 10-15cm. Hold 3 seconds. You should feel it in the glute, NOT the lower back. Lower. Complete 10 reps then switch. If you feel it in the back: slow down, focus on the glute squeeze before lifting.

ii.

Why It Works

The prone hip extension activates the gluteus maximus in isolation while the athlete is lying face-down — removing any possibility of compensating with the lower back or hamstrings. It is a pure glute firing drill used by rehabilitation specialists to re-establish the glute-first pattern that most hockey players lose from excessive sitting and skating in a forward-flexed position. When the glutes don't fire first, the lower back does — causing chronic back pain.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Isolates glute activation in prone position — corrects glute inhibition. Useful as part of a daily glute-activation routine. Cheap, takes 3 minutes, real payoff.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Lie face down"
  • "Lift leg straight up squeezing glute"
  • "Hold 2s"
  • "Lower slow"
v.

Common Mistakes

Lower back doing the work instead of the glute — the lift should be small and glute-driven. No squeeze before lifting — the pre-squeeze activates the glute before the movement. Lifting too high — beyond 15cm the lower back takes over. Rushing through it — this is activation work, not conditioning.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Prone hip extension with knee bent (isolates glute even more, removes hamstring).

Regression

Simply lying prone and squeezing both glutes simultaneously — begin activation before movement.
