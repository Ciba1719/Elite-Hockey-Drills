Terminal Knee Extension (TKE)

i.

How to Do It

Loop a resistance band around a fixed point at knee height behind you. Place the band behind your knee. Stand with the banded leg slightly bent. Stand on the banded leg — the band pulls the knee into flexion. Drive the knee to full extension against the band — contract the quad fully. Hold lockout 2 seconds. Release slowly back to the slight bend. 10-15 reps each leg. Feel it at the inner knee (VMO).

ii.

Why It Works

Terminal knee extension (TKE) with a band specifically targets the VMO (inner quad) and trains the full knee lockout pattern — the range most inhibited after knee injuries or surgery. In hockey players, the TKE restores proper quadriceps function at the end of the knee extension range, improves patellar tracking, and directly reduces anterior knee pain from heavy skating volume. It is the first exercise prescribed after ACL reconstruction for a reason.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Strengthens the VMO at terminal extension — prevents patellar tracking issues. Useful for anyone with cranky knees OR anyone trying to prevent cranky knees. Cheap weekly insurance.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Band behind knee"
  • "Stand on one leg"
  • "Fully extend knee against band resistance"
v.

Common Mistakes

Not reaching full extension — the terminal range is the whole point. Going too fast — slow and deliberate for maximum VMO activation. Band too loose — should provide meaningful resistance at full extension. Skipping the 2-second hold — the hold is where VMO fires hardest.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

Single-leg mini-squat (0-30° range) with band — adds load to the VMO activation pattern.

Regression

Seated quad sets (tighten quad while sitting, hold 5s) — most basic VMO activation.
