Foam Roll — Thoracic Spine

i.

How to Do It

Place the foam roller horizontally across your mid-back (T4-T8 level — between shoulder blades). Support your head with both hands behind the neck. Feet flat on the floor, knees bent. Allow your upper back to extend OVER the roller — let gravity do the work. Hold for 5 deep breaths, then shift the roller up 2-3 cm and repeat. Work from the mid-back up to just below the neck. Never roll the lower back.

ii.

Why It Works

Thoracic spine stiffness is epidemic in hockey players — the constant forward-flexed stick position for 60+ minutes per game fuses the T-spine into a rounded, kyphotic posture. A stiff T-spine forces the lumbar spine to compensate during rotation, which is a primary cause of lower-back pain and reduced shot power. 2 minutes of thoracic foam rolling unlocks the rotation that shot power requires.

iii.

Hockey Transfer

Opens the upper back — improves shooting posture and shoulder health. Daily 60-second drill. Improves overhead position, shot rotation, and posture in one shot.

iv.

Coaching Cues

  • "Roller under mid-back"
  • "Hands behind head"
  • "Drape back over roller"
  • "Work up the spine"
v.

Common Mistakes

Rolling the lower back — the lumbar spine should NOT be rolled. Thoracic only. Not letting the back extend — this requires you to actually relax over the roller. Moving too quickly — each segment needs 4-5 breaths of extension time. Skipping it pre-session — this is the most impactful mobility drill for shot power.

vi.

Progression / Regression

Progression

T-spine rotation on the roller (arms crossed, rotate side to side over the roller).

Regression

Seated thoracic extension over a chair back — less intense version for very stiff T-spines.
